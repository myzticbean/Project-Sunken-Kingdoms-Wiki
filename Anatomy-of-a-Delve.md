# Anatomy of a Delve

A **delve** is one run: from the knock at a ring to the moment the last delver walks out, or dawn.

```
  BELLMOUTH (surface)
      │  strike a ring's bell, fall in
      ▼
  ┌─────────────── HOLD I ── one kingdom ───────────────┐
  │ entrance ─ rooms ─ rooms ─ treasury ─ GATEHOUSE     │
  │ (free exit)                          (barred arch)  │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────┘
                                 win the objective, fall
  ┌─────────────── HOLD II ── another kingdom ▼─────────┐
  │ arrival arch ─ rooms ─ ... ─ treasury ─ GATEHOUSE   │
  └──────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────┘
                                             ▼
                              ... more holds at higher tiers ...
                                             ▼
  ┌─────────────── LAST HOLD ───────────────────────────┐
  │ arrival arch ─ rooms ─ treasury ─ processional ─ THRONE │
  └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## Holds

A **hold** is one sunken kingdom: a set of cave rooms on a grid, joined by tunnels. A delve is a
chain of holds, each in a different kingdom.

| Tier | Holds | Size |
|---|---|---|
| 1 The Undercroft | 2 | both small (3×3 grid, about 5 rooms) |
| 2 The Deep Halls | 3 | one small, then two full (5×5, about 11–15 rooms) |
| 3 The Ashen Reach | 4 | one small, then three full |

Hold I is always small. A **15-minute delve is exactly Hold I.**

**The layout is new every delve.** Room shapes, tunnels, which kingdoms you get and in what order,
where the elites stand: all of it is generated fresh when the delve opens and thrown away when it
closes. No route is ever walked twice.

**Inside a hold, nothing is locked.** Every tunnel is open. A hold is a branching network with loops,
dead ends and optional rooms, so beelining to the gate and sweeping every room are genuinely
different ways to play. The only gate is at the end.

**The next hold is built while you're in this one**, so there's no loading screen at the gate.

## Rooms

The rooms are **caverns with old masonry half-buried in them**. That's the intended look: these are
palaces the earth swallowed. Expect:

- terraces and ledges at different heights (a chest on a high ledge always has a way up)
- pools, some deep
- domed roofs, layered walls, floor levels
- ore veins in the walls
- ruined brick paving, broken columns
- **the companies' timber**: props, scaffolds, ladders, landings, bridges, old camps and their
  still-burning lanterns. Thick in the upper holds, thinning with depth, and absent from the Throne.
- the kingdom's hazard, always just off the safe path

Each room has a role. The **entrance** is where you land. The **treasury** is the deepest cave room,
with the hold's one **gilded chest**. Corridors, hubs and chambers lie between.

## The drop

**You never walk into a kingdom. You fall into it.**

Going down, through a ring or through a gate, puts you about 25 blocks above the floor of the next
hold's entrance room. A moment of darkness hides the shaft wall. Then the cavern opens up beneath you
and you drift down with Slow Falling while the Breath fills your hearts and the bell finishes tolling.

Coming back up is the opposite: you're set down **standing** on the threshold of an arch, or in your
ring. Descent happens to you. The way back is something you do.

## The gatehouse

Every hold except the last ends in a **gatehouse**, the one room in each hold that was *built*, not
carved. The kings built their Waygates as halls of dressed stone:

- A **paved processional** runs from the door to the gate, lit along its borders.
- **Two rows of columns** flank it, in the kingdom's own stone. Good cover to fight around.
- At the far end stands **the Waygate**: a 5×5 arch, **barred with iron**, with crying obsidian
  weeping on either side and **a bell** hung under the lintel, in reach from the floor.
- Two **unlit soul fires** flank the arch.
- **No loot.** The gatehouse is an arena.

When you enter, a purple bar appears and a title names the **objective**. See
[Gate Objectives](Gate-Objectives).

**Look through the arch.** A patch of the **next kingdom's rock** is set into the arch's frame, and
the last two rooms before the gate are already cut in the next kingdom's deep stone. You can see
what you're choosing before you choose it.

The pacing is deliberate: the **treasury** (the richest room) sits just before the gatehouse. You
take the gilded chest, then walk into a bare stone hall where the way on is barred.

## The Waygate: barred, then open

| State | Looks like | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|
| **Barred** | iron bars across the arch, fires unlit | — |
| **Opening** | the bars rise a course at a time like a portcullis, the fires light | iron door, then a clank per course, then the portal |
| **Open (onward)** | a black gateway fills the arch | — |
| **Open (homeward)** | the doorway fills with **blue** | — |

**Both ends are real.** Cross a Waygate and you land beside a matching **arrival arch** in the next
hold. It's never barred (you opened it), its fires are lit and it has a bell. Crossed Waygates are
**two-way**: you can walk back up through them, all the way to Hold I.

## The Throne

The last hold has no gatehouse. Its deepest room is **the Throne**: the king's crypt.

- You approach through a **processional** tunnel: paved, hung with soul lanterns on chains, banners
  in the kingdom's colour on both walls.
- As you near it, a **hum** begins: the bell resonating every two seconds, leading you toward the
  hall.
- At the hall's door, the hum **stops**. The silence is the cue.
- Inside: an aisle between columns hung with banners, an honour guard of armoured statues, candles,
  braziers, and a **tiered dais** with a throne and the **King's Bell** hanging over the king's stand.

See [Kings, Seals and Tiers](Kings-Seals-and-Tiers).

## The Tithe *(planned)*

On the far side of every crossing, beside the arrival arch: an offering table with **three item
frames**. Take one. The other two sink into the stone. The choice is a **blessing** that lasts the
whole delve, a finished **heirloom**, or a bundle of the kingdom's **goods**. At least one is always a
blessing.

Possible blessings:

| Blessing | Effect |
|---|---|
| the Watchful Eye | Night Vision; wights glow from twice as far |
| the Heavy Hand | Strength I |
| the Quick Foot | Speed I |
| the Deep Breath | +4 hearts |
| the Iron Skin | Resistance I |
| the Keen Edge | +2 attack damage |
| the Long Silence | the Waking runs a quarter slower |
| the Full Purse | one extra roll in every hoard |
| the Reaper's Due | putting down the dead heals double |

Blessings **stack** across crossings, so a four-hold delve gives you a build you didn't plan and will
never draw the same way again. Nothing carries over to the next delve.

## Time limit

A delve lasts at most **90 minutes from the first fall**. That's the night. At dawn the way closes.
You get warnings at 10 minutes and 2 minutes. This is a ceiling, not a pace: most delves end long
before it. See [Death, Remains and Dawn](Death-Remains-and-Dawn).

## Parties

A delve is a **membership list**. You join by falling through the ring. You leave the list one way:
by walking out through a homeward gate.

- The ring stays open the whole delve, so **latecomers can fall in** and catch up through the opened
  Waygates.
- Everyone shares **one Waking clock** and one bossbar.
- Each extra member adds an extra roll to every hoard (up to four), and makes the dead tougher (+50%
  health per extra member, capped at 3.5×).
- The delve ends when the list is empty, or at dawn. **Not** when everyone is dead: a party lying
  dead at the hearth still has a delve running below, its remains waiting and its clock rising.
