# The Story of the Sunken Kingdoms

*What follows is the story as Bellmouth tells it. Every rule of the game comes from a line in it. If
something below seems strange, the reason is somewhere in this story.*

---

## The oath and the sinking

Long ago the kingdoms of the world swore an oath together. Nobody in Bellmouth remembers what it
said, only that some kingdoms broke it.

The ones that broke it were **sunk beneath the earth**. They did not burn or fall to an army. The
ground opened under them and took them whole: halls, courts, treasuries, kings and all. Then the
earth heaped itself over the grave. That heap is **the Cairn**, the great mountain at the centre of
the valley.

The kingdoms did not sink all at once. They went down **one after another**. The first, and the
richest, went deepest. Their kings took their treasuries down with them, and their dead have had the
longest to gather. The last ones fell nearest the surface, and they were the poorest. So the deeper
you go, the older the kingdom, the greater its hoard, and the worse the dead who keep it.

The rock is still moving. Thorns grow in the green halls, spikes of stone hang from the masons'
vaults, sculk creeps through the silent court, and fire still burns under the ashen one.

## The curse that keeps out armies

The sunken kingdoms were cursed **never to be mapped**. No road below is walked twice. Each time a
gate is opened, the halls rearrange themselves. When it closes, they fall back into the dark.

Bring a map and it stays blank. Send an army and it gets lost. Only **small bands** can go down, find
their way by eye and come back. That is why Bellmouth has delvers and not soldiers.

## Held exactly as they were

The halls are **held exactly as they were the night they sank**. Nothing rusts down there and nothing
mends. No wall gives way to a pick. No stone can be placed. Your sword does not dull and your armour
does not wear. Your wounds don't close either.

The one exception is **ore**. A vein can be worked, once. Then it turns to plain rock and stays that
way.

The kingdoms' **heirlooms** were held the same way. A blade carried up from a king's treasury never
breaks, above or below. No anvil, grindstone or smithing table on the surface can mend it, change it
or make it greater. It is exactly what it was.

## The Breath

When the kingdoms went down, **the dead took the breath of the living** with them. The halls are
still thick with it.

When you fall in, the Breath fills you past what a body can hold above: **ten extra hearts**. But
nothing down there is alive, so there is nothing to give breath back to you. Rest does not restore
it. Food does not restore it. Only one thing does: **putting down one of the dead**. The breath it
carried returns to whoever laid it to rest.

So below, avoiding fights is how you run out.

## Nothing below is alive

This is the most important thing Bellmouth knows: **nothing below is alive, so nothing below can
die.**

The dead can only be **put down**. They lie where they fell until the gate closes, and
when the next gate opens they rise where they lay. A king is no different. His crypt is the one room
the halls never lose, and when you come back tomorrow he will be on his throne again.

The dead **lie under their own floors**. They don't walk in from somewhere else. When they come,
they come *up*, out of the stone at your feet.

The dead **keep their halls**. One that strays too far from its room forgets the chase and sinks
back into the floor.

The dead **sleep lightly**. A broken gate wakes the hold beyond it. Lingering wakes them slowly.
Robbing them wakes them fast.

The dead **keep their hoards**. Open one while its keepers still stand and they know. They rise
around it, and the hold stirs.

A king's **household** wore his marks in life and keeps them in death. Some of the dead bear a mark:
swift, iron-bound, unresting, wrathful, or hidden in shadow.

## The Sunken Bell

When the kingdoms fell, their heirs escaped with one thing: the **upper half of the great bell**.
They carried it out and hung it in the first house built at the mountain's mouth. That house became
the tavern, and the town that grew around it became **Bellmouth**.

The **lower half** never came up. It hangs in every gate below. And the founders carried up
smaller bells from the gates too. Each one still answers the gate it was cut from. Those bells hang
over the **rings** in the Mouth of the Cairn.

**They are one bell.** Strike any part of it and the others answer. Strike a bell over a ring and
something deep below rings back: one toll for every step down, and then the ground gives way.

The bell was always rung to show the dead. When the dead stir, the bell tolls, and in that moment you
can see them glowing through the rock.

A bell answers **one call at a time**. One ring, one delve. And the dead stir for a footfall, not a
bell: the night below begins when the first delver lands, not when the bell rings. If nobody falls
in, the ground closes again.

## The Waygates and their gatekeepers

Each kingdom sank **whole**. Inside one, the rock is all one rock, and its tunnels join hall to hall.
But between kingdoms there is nothing, only dark. The only bridges are the **Waygates** the old kings
built between their courts: great arches of dressed stone, each in its own gatehouse.

Every Waygate was **sworn shut** by its gatekeepers and barred with iron. To cross, you must break
that oath: put the gatekeepers down, or stand your ground while the gate opens.

A Waygate is an arch, and you can see through it. The far kingdom's rock shows in the arch's frame,
so you know where you are going before you choose to go.

And because the kingdoms are *underneath*, not behind, **every gate is a drop**. You don't step
through to the next kingdom. You fall into it.

## The way home

The gate you opened from above is **bound to your bell**. It answers to you, so you can always walk
out the way you came in.

Any other gate means **striking its bell** to call it home. The whole hold hears that. The dead come
up in waves, and in the deeper kingdoms the **Oathbound** come too: the kings' sworn knights and
their hounds, who answer the bell. Hold out until the bell has finished tolling and the gate turns
toward home.

A king's own bell **tolls itself for his death**. When he is put down, his bell rings once and the
way home opens on his stand before the throne. Nothing below is left to hold it shut.

A gate holds **for one night**. At dawn the way closes, and whatever is still below stays below.

## What is yours and what is theirs

The Breath goes home when your body does, and it takes with it **only what is yours**. Whatever you
brought down, you keep. Whatever you *found* belongs to the kingdom until you walk it out under your
own power.

What belongs to a kingdom may pass from hand to hand below. But the kingdom's own chests, barrels and
frames **will not hold it**, because the dead would only take it back. A hoard emptied means nothing
to the dead, and the halls let it crumble to dust.

A delver who falls is **left where they fell**, and the halls hold that as they hold everything else.
Your bones and what you carried wait there until dawn, or until you fall again.

Once the Breath has gone home, **it does not come back down that night**. Walk out and your night
below is done. Fall in battle and it isn't. You wake at the hearth, and the ring still answers you.

## The Seals

The deeper Waygates open only to one who bears the **Seal of the king above**. A Seal is not taken.
The signet stays on the king's hand. Each delver standing in the crypt presses their own Seal from
it. There is nothing to fight over, and nothing to carry for a friend who wasn't there.

## Bellmouth

Nothing has ever come up out of the Cairn but what a delver **carried in their hands**. No cart, no
rope, no army. That is why there is a town of delvers at the mountain's mouth, not a mine.

The **factors** never go down. They pay in **Crowns**, struck from the kingdoms' own gold, a hundred
to the bar. A factor deals only in what has been brought up before. When a new kind of thing comes up
out of the Cairn for the first time, a new factor comes to town to trade in it. No factor has ever
named a price for a Seal or an heirloom. There is none.

**The only fire left in the world burns under the Cairn.** There is no Nether and no End. Brewing
stock and black steel come up out of the holds, or not at all.

## The companies

Word of a sinking always ran ahead of it. **Companies** came to strip each kingdom as it failed. They
shored up its halls with timber, raised scaffolds where floors had dropped, laid landings over the
rising water, and camped in its halls to hold what they took.

On the night each kingdom sank, it took its company down with it.

Their works are held like everything else: their props and ladders and lanterns stand exactly as they
stood that night. Nothing rots. What the sinking broke stays broken. Their lanterns still burn. The
first kingdoms went down before anyone thought to follow them, so the deepest halls have no timber at
all. By the last sinking, every company in the world was there, so the upper holds are full of it.

Bellmouth remembers them. It goes down with only what a delver can carry.

## The last faithful

*(Planned.)* The last faithful kept **vigil fires** at the gates. A gate still opens for the living
who keep the vigil.

A **vow** sworn at the ring binds the descent, and the dead honour an oath.

Every court leaves a **tithe** at its gate for the dead. The dead let a delver take one thing from it,
and no more.

### How the lore shapes the game

The project has one firm design rule: **every mechanic must trace to a line of the lore.** A rule
with no line in the story is treated as wrong. Some examples:

| The story says | So the game does |
|---|---|
| The halls rearrange each time a gate opens | Every delve is freshly generated and thrown away after |
| The halls cannot be mapped | Maps render blank below |
| The oldest kingdoms sank deepest | Loot gets better the deeper you go |
| Nothing there mends or gives way to a pick | No natural regeneration, no block placing or breaking, no durability loss |
| The dead took the breath | +10 hearts on arrival, and putting the dead down heals you |
| The dead lie under their floors | Enemies never pop in; they rise out of the ground |
| The dead keep their halls | Enemies give up a chase and sink if lured too far |
| A bell was rung to show the dead | Each toll makes nearby enemies glow |
| Nothing below is alive | Kings return every delve. Text always says *put down*, never *kill* |
| Only what you carry comes out | Found loot is tagged and lost at dawn; extraction is on foot |
| A gate holds for one night | A delve ends 90 minutes after the first fall |
| The only fire is under the Cairn | The Nether and the End are switched off |
