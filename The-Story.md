# The Story of the Sunken Kingdoms

*This is the tale as Bellmouth tells it, late, under the Upper Half, when the fire has burned low and
nobody is going down again tonight. Every law of the halls below is written somewhere in it. If
something down there seems strange, the reason is here.*

> *Nothing has ever come up out of the Cairn but what a delver carried in their hands.*
>
> — said at the hearth

---

## I. The Oath and the Sinking

There was an oath, once. Every kingdom of the world swore it. Nobody living remembers the words.
Bellmouth remembers only what became of the kingdoms that broke it.

They were not conquered. They were not burned. No army ever marched on them.

**The ground opened.**

It opened under their courts and their markets and their throne rooms, and it took them **whole**:
halls and hoards, kings and crowns, every soul inside the walls. It swallowed them into the dark.
Then the earth heaped itself over the grave, stone upon stone, until a mountain stood where the
kingdoms had been.

That mountain is **the Cairn**. It is a tomb, and it is still settling.

They did not go down together. They sank **one after another**, across long years, each falling
further than the one after. The first to break the oath went first and went deepest: the oldest
courts, the richest crowns, their treasuries clutched to their chests as the ground closed over their
heads. Their dead have had the longest to gather. The last kingdoms to fall lie nearest the surface,
and they were the poorest of all.

So the rule is old, and it is simple:

**The deeper you go, the older the kingdom, the greater the hoard, and the worse the dead who keep
it.**

And the rock has never stopped moving. Thorn still creeps through the drowned forest. Teeth of stone
still hang from the masons' vaults. Sculk still spreads through the silent court, listening. And
beneath all of it, fire still burns where one kingdom burned all the way down into the underworld.

## II. The Curse of the Unmapped

The sinking was not the whole of the sentence. The fallen kingdoms were cursed **never to be
mapped**.

No road below is walked twice. Each time a gate opens, the halls rearrange themselves in the dark:
tunnels finding new rooms, rooms turning their faces away. When the gate closes, all of it falls back
into nothing.

Carry a map below and it stays blank, as if the paper itself refuses to remember. March an army in
and it will lose itself in the first hour. The Cairn admits only **small bands**: a handful of souls
with sharp eyes and steady nerve, who find their way by torchlight and climb out the same night.

That is why Bellmouth is a town of delvers, and not of soldiers.

## III. Held as They Fell

Below, time stopped.

The halls are **held exactly as they were the night they sank**. Nothing rusts. Nothing rots.
Nothing mends. No wall yields to a pick, and no stone can be laid on stone. Your blade will not dull
down there. Your armour will not wear thin.

**And your wounds will not close.**

Only the **veins** in the rock can be worked, and only once. Strike one and it gives up what it
holds, then turns to dead stone for good.

The kingdoms' **heirlooms** were held the same way. A blade lifted from a sunken king's treasury
will never break: not below, not above, not ever. No anvil can mend it. No grindstone can strip it.
No smith alive can make it greater. It is exactly what it was the night its kingdom fell, and so it
will always be.

## IV. The Breath

When the kingdoms went down, **the dead took the breath of the living** with them.

All of it. The last gasp of every soul who sank. The halls are still thick with it, heavy as fog in a
crypt.

Fall into the Cairn and you breathe it in. It floods you past anything a living body can hold above:
**ten hearts more** than you carried down. You feel it surge through you before your boots ever touch
stone.

But there is a price written into it. Nothing below is alive, so nothing below can give breath back.
Rest will not restore it. Food will not restore it. Only one thing will:

**Put down one of the dead, and the breath it stole comes home to you.**

Below, the one who hides bleeds out slowly. The one who fights keeps breathing.

## V. Nothing Below Is Alive

Here is the first truth of Bellmouth, older than the town itself:

> **Nothing below is alive. So nothing below can die.**

The dead can only be **put down**. They lie where they fell until the gate closes, and when the next
gate opens, they rise where they lay. Kings are no different. A king's crypt is the one room the
halls never lose, and the king you put down tonight will be waiting on his throne tomorrow.

The dead **lie beneath their own floors**. They do not walk in from somewhere else. When they come for
you, they come **up**: a skull, then shoulders, breaking through the stone at your feet with the soil
still falling from them.

The dead **keep their halls**. Lure one too far from the room it guards and it forgets you entirely,
and sinks back into the floor as though it had never risen.

The dead **sleep lightly**. A broken gate wakes the kingdom beyond it. Lingering wakes them slowly.
Greed wakes them fast.

The dead **keep their hoards**. Lay a hand on one while its keepers still stand and they *know*. They
rise around it, and the whole hold stirs.

And a king's **household** wore his marks in life, and wears them still. Some of the dead bear them:
the swift, the iron-bound, the unresting, the wrathful, and the ones that move unseen in shadow.

## VI. The Sunken Bell

When the end came, the heirs of the fallen kingdoms escaped with a single thing: the **upper half of
the great bell**.

They dragged it up out of the dark and hung it in the first house they raised at the mountain's
mouth. That house became the tavern. The town that grew around it took its name from what hung
inside: **Bellmouth**.

The **lower half** never came up. It hangs in every gate below: in every arch, in every kingdom, all
at once.

The founders carried up lesser bells as well, cut from the gates themselves. Each still answers the
gate it was torn from. They hang now above the **rings** in the Mouth of the Cairn.

**And they are all one bell.** Strike any part of it, and every other part answers.

Strike a bell above a ring and listen. First, silence. Then, from far below, the answer: one toll
for every step down into the dark, each deeper than the last, climbing up the shaft toward you. Then
thunder.

**Then the ground gives way.**

The bell was always rung to show the dead. When they stir, it tolls, and for one heartbeat you can see
them, burning through the rock like coals.

A bell answers **one call at a time**: one ring, one delve. And the dead stir for a footfall, not for
a bell. The night below begins only when the first delver lands. If no one falls, the ground closes
again, and waits.

## VII. The Waygates

Each kingdom sank **whole**. Inside one, the rock is all one rock, and its tunnels run hall to hall.
But between kingdoms there is nothing. Only the dark.

The only bridges are the **Waygates** the old kings built between their courts: great arches of
dressed stone, each standing in its own gatehouse like an altar.

Every Waygate was **sworn shut** by its gatekeepers and barred with iron. To pass, you must break
that oath for them: put the gatekeepers down, or stand your ground while the gate is forced.

A Waygate is an arch, and an arch can be seen through. The far kingdom's stone shows in its frame,
so you always know what waits on the other side before you choose to go.

And because the kingdoms lie *beneath* and not *beyond*, **every gate is a drop**. You do not step
through into the next kingdom.

**You fall into it.**

## VIII. The Way Home

The gate you opened from above is **bound to your bell**. It will always answer you. You can always
walk out the way you came in.

Every other gate must be **called home** by striking its bell. And when you strike it, **the whole
hold hears**.

The dead come up in waves. In the deeper kingdoms the **Oathbound** come with them: the kings' sworn
knights and their great hounds, who still answer the bell as they did in life. Hold your ground until
the last toll fades, and the gate will turn toward home.

A king's own bell **tolls itself for his fall**. When he is put down it rings once, deep enough to
feel in your teeth, and the way home opens on the stand before his throne. Nothing below is left to
hold it shut.

But no gate holds forever. A gate holds **for one night**. At dawn the way closes, and whatever is
still below stays below.

## IX. What Is Yours, and What Is Theirs

The Breath goes home when your body does, and it carries **only what is yours**.

Whatever you brought down, you keep. Whatever you *found* belongs to the kingdom until you walk it
out under your own power.

The kingdom's things may pass from hand to hand below. But the kingdom's own chests and barrels and
frames **will not hold them**: the dead would only take them back. And a hoard once emptied means
nothing to the dead. The halls let it crumble into dust.

A delver who falls is **left where they fell**, and the halls hold them as they hold everything else.
Your bones, and everything you carried, will wait for you there, until dawn or until you fall again.

Once your breath has gone home, **it does not come back down that night**. Walk out, and your night
below is over. Fall in battle, and it is not. You wake at the hearth, and the ring still answers.

## X. The Seals

The deeper Waygates open only to one who bears the **Seal of the king above**.

A Seal is not taken. The signet never leaves the dead king's hand. Each delver standing in the crypt
when he falls presses their own Seal from it. There is nothing to fight over, and nothing to carry for
a friend who was not there.

## XI. Bellmouth

Nothing has ever come up out of the Cairn except what a delver **carried in their own hands**. No
cart. No rope. No army.

That is why a town of delvers stands at the mountain's mouth, and not a mine.

The **factors** never go down. They pay in **Crowns**, struck from the sunken kingdoms' own gold, a
hundred to the bar. And a factor deals only in what has been brought up before. When a new treasure
rises out of the Cairn for the first time, a new factor comes to Bellmouth to trade in it.

No factor has ever named a price for a Seal or an heirloom. **There is none.**

**The only fire left in the world burns under the Cairn.** There is no Nether. There is no End.
Whatever burns, whatever brews, whatever black steel is worked: it comes up out of the holds, or it
does not come at all.

## XII. The Companies

Word of a sinking always ran ahead of it.

When a kingdom began to fail, the **companies** came, to strip it bare while it still stood. They
shored its cracking halls with timber. They raised scaffolds where its floors had dropped away. They
laid landings over the black water rising from below. They pitched their camps in its halls and held
what they took.

**And on the night each kingdom sank, it took its company down with it.**

Their works are held like everything else. Every prop, every ladder, every scaffold stands exactly as
it stood that night. Nothing rots. What the sinking broke stays broken. **Their lanterns are still
burning.**

The first kingdoms went down before anyone thought to follow them, so the deepest halls hold no
timber at all. By the last sinking, every company in the world was there, and the upper holds are
thick with their beams.

Bellmouth remembers them. Bellmouth goes down with only what a delver can carry.

## XIII. The Last Faithful *(planned)*

The last faithful kept **vigil fires** at the gates. A gate will still open for the living who keep
the vigil.

A **vow** sworn at the ring binds the descent, and the dead honour an oath.

And every court left a **tithe** at its gate for the dead. The dead will let a delver take one thing
from it.

One. And no more.

## How the lore shapes the game

The project has one firm design rule: **every mechanic must trace to a line of the lore.** A rule with
no line in the story is treated as wrong. Some examples:

| The story says | So the game does |
|---|---|
| The halls rearrange each time a gate opens | Every delve is freshly generated and thrown away after |
| The halls cannot be mapped | Maps render blank below |
| The oldest kingdoms sank deepest | Loot gets better the deeper you go |
| Nothing there mends or gives way to a pick | No natural regeneration, no block placing or breaking, no durability loss |
| The dead took the breath | +10 hearts on arrival, and putting the dead down heals you |
| The dead lie under their floors | Enemies never pop in; they rise out of the ground |
| The dead keep their halls | Enemies give up a chase and sink if lured too far |
| A bell was rung to show the dead | Each toll makes nearby enemies glow |
| Nothing below is alive | Kings return every delve. Text always says *put down*, never *kill* |
| Only what you carry comes out | Found loot is tagged and lost at dawn; extraction is on foot |
| A gate holds for one night | A delve ends 90 minutes after the first fall |
| The only fire is under the Cairn | The Nether and the End are switched off |
