# Bellmouth and the Cairn

A town dug into the foot of a tomb. A bell that hangs in the tavern and rings, unbidden, whenever
someone dies below. A gate in the mountain's face, a tunnel behind it, and a well at the bottom
waiting to open.

Bellmouth is where you trade, brew, gather and rest. The Cairn is where everything else happens.

## The valley

The world above is a green Minecraft valley, closed in by a **world border** drawn tight round the
Cairn's foot and the town. The valley is held: nothing in it breaks, and nothing is placed, so
Bellmouth stays as it was built. Its trees are its own, oaks and spruces grown for this valley,
no two alike.

What is gone is the **Nether and the End**. In the lore, the only fire left in the world burns under
the Cairn. In practice, the delve replaces the vanilla "beat the dragon" arc: the tiers below *are*
the progression.

## The Cairn

The Cairn is the mountain at the heart of the valley: the heap the earth raised over the sunken
kingdoms. It always rises in green country, never in badlands, mesa, savanna or desert.
Nobody digs into the Cairn. The only way in is the gate.

## The gate and the tunnel

Above the town, a **gate** is carved into the Cairn's face: an old arch, overgrown. Behind it a
worn tunnel curves down into the mountain, lit by copper lanterns hung on chains, to the Mouth.

## The Mouth

The **Mouth** is a cave hall inside the Cairn, carved like the holds below, which a thousand years
of delvers have made their own: carved pillars with a lamp on each, benches facing the well,
banners faded and torn, offerings at the well's edge, the companies' names cut one to a sign, and a
floor worn smooth along the line everyone walks from the tunnel. At its heart is **the ring**:

- The ring is a **well**: a grated shaft with a cap of stone over it, under an arch that faces the
  way in.
- A **bell** hangs in the arch. It was carried up out of the Cairn by a founder, and it still
  answers the gate it was cut from.
- Floating text over the bell shows **which tier** the ring is set to.
- The hall's **candles** stand cold. Every toll of the bell lights more of them, from the well
  outward, and they go out when the ground closes.

The Mouth is held like the halls below: you can't break or build anything inside it.

**One delve at a time.** While a party is below, the ring is taken. Strike it and you get a single
dull toll and the message *"The bell is already answered."*

## The tavern and the hearth

You wake in **the tavern**, where the paths meet below the gate:

- **The Upper Half** hangs over the spot where you wake, on copper chains from the roof, down
  through an open gallery: the top half of the great bell, the first thing the heirs carried out.
- **The wall**: three couplets that tell you what to do, plus the **records** (deepest hold,
  fastest Throne for each tier, highest band anyone walked out at) and the latest extractions by
  name, cut into the panels either side of the doors.
- **The factors**: the traders who deal with delvers, in a row with the Bellringer at its end.
  See [Crowns and Factors](Crowns-and-Factors.md).

When you first join you get a short book, *The Wall*, with the same three couplets:

> *Strike the bell above a ring,*
> *and fall to the halls of a sunken king.*
> *Below, the arch bell tolls you down;*
> *a broken gate tolls you home to town.*
> *What you bear up is yours alone;*
> *what you fall with waits beside your bones.*

## The Upper Half

The Upper Half is one bell with every bell below. Anything that happens down there, it hears:

| The Upper Half... | When |
|---|---|
| rings once, bright and high | someone walks out through a homeward gate (**homecoming**) |
| rings once, low, unbidden | someone falls below (**the knell**) |
| swings with every toll | someone is fighting their way out through a Holdout (**the peal**) |

So anyone sitting in the tavern can follow the night below just by listening.

## The town itself

Bellmouth is raised with the valley, at the Cairn's foot. Every factor's house is its own,
dug into the foothills the way a builder would set it: its front out in timber and stone, its back
cut into the rock, its roof running into the hill. Pell's jettied shop, Ansel's stone assay house,
Aldith's funnel house with its glowing vapour, and Sigrun's forge, which waits for its smith. Roads
join every door, the tavern and the gate, cut into the hill where it is steep and climbing on
stairs.
